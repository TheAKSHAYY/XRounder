## 📌 Section A: High-Yield Very Short Answer Questions (2 Marks Each)

### Q1. [2 Marks] What is a Budget according to CIMA London?
**Model Answer:**
CIMA defines a budget as: *'A financial and/or quantitative statement, prepared and approved prior to a defined period of time, of the policy to be pursued during that period for the purpose of attaining a given objective.'*

### Q2. [2 Marks] Differentiate between a Budget and a Forecast.
**Model Answer:**
- **Forecast**: An objective probabilistic estimate of future events over which management has no control.
- **Budget**: A committed managerial target and operating plan that management actively intends to attain.

### Q3. [2 Marks] What is a Flexible Budget?
**Model Answer:**
A Flexible Budget recognizes cost behavior patterns (fixed, variable, semi-variable) and automatically recalculates budgeted cost allowances for any achieved volume or capacity level (e.g., 60%, 80%, 100%).

### Q4. [2 Marks] What is Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB) and who introduced it?
**Model Answer:**
Introduced by **Peter Pyhrr** at Texas Instruments in 1969, ZBB requires every manager to justify every proposed expenditure from a clean slate (zero base) for each new period, rejecting incremental historical budgeting.

### Q5. [2 Marks] What is a Decision Package in Zero-Based Budgeting?
**Model Answer:**
A formal operational document that identifies a discrete program or activity, evaluates alternatives, assesses consequences of non-approval, and requests specific financial resources.

### Q6. [2 Marks] What is a 'Principal / Key Budget Factor'?
**Model Answer:**
A limiting constraint (such as market sales demand, scarce raw material supply, or machine capacity) that dictates the maximum operational scale of the organization and serves as the starting point for all other functional budgets.

### Q7. [2 Marks] State the components of Prime Cost in a Cost Sheet.
**Model Answer:**
$$\text{Prime Cost} = \text{Direct Materials Consumed} + \text{Direct Wages} + \text{Direct Chargeable Expenses}$$

### Q8. [2 Marks] How is a Tender / Quotation Price formulated?
**Model Answer:**
$$\text{Tender Price} = \text{Estimated Cost of Production} + \text{Estimated S&D Overheads} + \text{Desired Profit Margin}$$
Overhead absorption rates are computed from past cost sheets.

### Q1. [5 Marks] Explain Fixed Budget vs Flexible Budget with a Comparison Table.
**Model Answer:**
| Dimension | Fixed Budget | Flexible Budget |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Capacity Level** | Prepared for only ONE rigid activity level | Prepared for MULTIPLE capacity levels (60%, 80%, 100%) |
| **Cost Behavior** | Treats all costs as rigid lumps | Segregates costs into Fixed, Variable, and Semi-Variable |
| **Variance Analysis** | Misleading volume distortions when actual volume differs | Meaningful 'like-with-like' operational comparison |
| **Applicability** | Stable, highly predictable business environments | Dynamic, fluctuating market conditions |

### Q2. [5 Marks] Explain the Step-by-Step Procedure of Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB).
**Model Answer:**
### 4 Stages of Zero-Based Budgeting
1. **Identification of Decision Units**: Dividing organization into autonomous decision units (departments, product lines, R&D projects).
2. **Formulation of Decision Packages**: For each unit, managers draft packages specifying: objectives, feasibility, cost-benefit analysis, alternative approaches, and impact of zero funding.
3. **Evaluation and Ranking of Packages**: Management committee ranks decision packages in order of strategic ROI and organizational priority.
4. **Resource Allocation**: Available capital is allocated downward from rank 1 until funds are exhausted; unranked packages are eliminated.

### Q3. [5 Marks] Explain the Structure and Flow of a Comprehensive Cost Sheet.
**Model Answer:**
### Standard Structure of a Cost Sheet

```text
  Opening Stock of Raw Materials
+ Purchases of Raw Materials
+ Carriage Inward
- Closing Stock of Raw Materials
---------------------------------------------------------------
= RAW MATERIALS CONSUMED
+ Direct Labor / Wages
+ Direct Expenses
---------------------------------------------------------------
= PRIME COST
+ Factory / Works Overheads (Rent, power, supervisor, machine dep)
+ Opening Work-in-Progress (WIP)
- Closing Work-in-Progress (WIP)
---------------------------------------------------------------
= WORKS / FACTORY COST
+ Office & Administrative Overheads (Staff salaries, audit fees, rent)
---------------------------------------------------------------
= COST OF PRODUCTION
+ Opening Stock of Finished Goods
- Closing Stock of Finished Goods
---------------------------------------------------------------
= COST OF GOODS SOLD (COGS)
+ Selling & Distribution Overheads (Salesmen commission, delivery, ads)
---------------------------------------------------------------
= TOTAL COST / COST OF SALES
+ PROFIT
---------------------------------------------------------------
= SELLING PRICE (TOTAL SALES VALUE)
```

### Q4. [5 Marks] Explain the Cash Budget: Methods of Preparation and Managerial Significance.
**Model Answer:**
### Cash Budgeting
A Cash Budget projects periodic cash receipts and disbursements to maintain liquidity and prevent cash starvation.

### Methods of Preparation:
1. **Receipts and Payments Method** (Most common): Categorizes cash inflows (cash sales, debtor collections, asset sales) and cash outflows (purchases, wages, expenses, capital expenditure).
2. **Adjusted Profit and Loss Method**: Adjusts budgeted net profit for non-cash items (depreciation) and working capital shifts.
3. **Balance Sheet Projection Method**.

**Significance**: Signals surplus cash for short-term investment or warns of impending cash deficits requiring overdraft credit arrangements.

### 2. Tender Quotation for Supplying 200 Units in 2024
**Given Conditions for Tender:**
- Estimated Raw Material per unit: Rs. 220
- Estimated Direct Wages per unit: Rs. 160
- Factory Overheads absorbed as **% of Direct Wages**.
- Office Overheads absorbed as **% of Works Cost**.
- Selling Overheads absorbed as **% of Works Cost**.
- Desired Profit: **20% on Selling Price**.

### Step-by-Step Overhead Recovery Computations:
1. Factory Overhead % on Wages: $(75,000 / 1,50,000) \times 100 = \mathbf{50\%}$
2. Office Overhead % on Works Cost: $(95,000 / 4,75,000) \times 100 = \mathbf{20\%}$
3. Selling Overhead % on Works Cost: $(57,000 / 4,75,000) \times 100 = \mathbf{12\%}$

### Tender Statement (200 Units):

| Cost Elements for 200 Units | Amount (Rs.) |
| :--- | :---: |
| Direct Materials ($200 \times 220$) | 44,000 |
| Direct Wages ($200 \times 160$) | 32,000 |
| **Prime Cost** | **76,000** |
| Factory Overheads ($50\% \text{ of Wages } 32,000$) | 16,000 |
| **Works Cost** | **92,000** |
| Office Overheads ($20\% \text{ of Works Cost } 92,000$) | 18,400 |
| **Cost of Production** | **1,10,400** |
| Selling Overheads ($12\% \text{ of Works Cost } 92,000$) | 11,040 |

- Administrative Overhead: Rs. 20,000 (80% Fixed, 20% Variable)

### Per Unit Variable Cost & Fixed Base Breakdown:
1. **Material**: $60,000 / 6,000 = \mathbf{Rs. 10/unit}$.
2. **Labor**: $30,000 / 6,000 = \mathbf{Rs. 5/unit}$.
3. **Factory Overhead**:
   - Fixed: $40\% \text{ of } 40,000 = \mathbf{Rs. 16,000}$ (Constant).

   - Fixed: $80\% \text{ of } 20,000 = \mathbf{Rs. 16,000}$ (Constant).

5. **Selling Overhead**:
   - Fixed: $50\% \text{ of } 10,000 = \mathbf{Rs. 5,000}$ (Constant).
   - Variable: $50\% \text{ of } 10,000 = 5,000 / 6,000 = \mathbf{Rs. 0.83/unit}$.
