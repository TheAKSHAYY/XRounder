## 📌 Section A: High-Yield Very Short Answer Questions (2 Marks Each)
*Target: Write precise, definition-focused answers in 2–3 sentences or structured tables to secure maximum marks.*

### Q1. [2 Marks] Define Accounting according to the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA).
**Model Answer:**
The AICPA defines accounting as: *'The art of recording, classifying, and summarizing in a significant manner and in terms of money, transactions and events which are, in part at least, of a financial character, and interpreting the results thereof.'*

### Q2. [2 Marks] What is the Business Entity Concept?
**Model Answer:**
The Business Entity concept dictates that a commercial business is treated as a completely separate and distinct legal entity from its proprietors/owners. Consequently, capital invested by the owner is recorded as an internal liability of the firm.

### Q3. [2 Marks] Explain the Going Concern Concept.
**Model Answer:**
The Going Concern concept assumes that the enterprise will continue operating indefinitely into the foreseeable future and has neither the intention nor the necessity of liquidation or drastically curtailing its operations.

### Q4. [2 Marks] What is the fundamental Accounting Equation?
**Model Answer:**
$$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Owner's Capital (Equity)}$$
Every transaction affects both sides equally, maintaining mathematical equilibrium.

### Q5. [2 Marks] State the Golden Rules of Accounting under the Traditional (British) approach.
**Model Answer:**
1. **Personal Accounts**: Debit the receiver, Credit the giver.
2. **Real Accounts**: Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out.
3. **Nominal Accounts**: Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains.

### Q6. [2 Marks] State the Rules of Debit and Credit under the Modern (American) approach.
**Model Answer:**
- **Assets & Expenses/Losses**: Increase $\implies$ **DEBIT**; Decrease $\implies$ **CREDIT**.
- **Liabilities, Capital & Revenues/Gains**: Increase $\implies$ **CREDIT**; Decrease $\implies$ **DEBIT**.

### Q7. [2 Marks] What is a Contra Entry in a three-column cash book?
**Model Answer:**
A Contra Entry (marked with 'C' in L.F.) occurs when a transaction simultaneously affects both Cash and Bank columns (e.g., cash deposited into bank or cash withdrawn from bank for office use).

### Q8. [2 Marks] Explain the Imprest System of Petty Cash.
**Model Answer:**
Under the Imprest System, the petty cashier is advanced a fixed sum (float) at the start of a period. At period end, after verifying expense vouchers, the chief cashier reimburses the exact amount spent, restoring the original float.

### Q1. [5 Marks] Compare Accounting Concepts vs Accounting Conventions with a Structured Table.
**Model Answer:**
| Dimension | Accounting Concepts (Assumptions) | Accounting Conventions (Practices) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Fundamental theoretical assumptions and postulates upon which accounting is founded | Customs, ethical guidelines, and traditions evolved through established usage |
| **Authority** | Enforced strictly by statutory GAAP and standard bodies | Followed to ensure consistency, clarity, and conservative prudence |
| **Bias / Discretion** | Objective and mathematical; no personal bias | Involves managerial judgment (e.g., degree of conservatism) |
| **Core Examples** | - Going Concern Concept<br>- Business Entity Concept<br>- Money Measurement Concept<br>- Dual Aspect Concept | - Convention of Conservatism (Prudence)<br>- Convention of Consistency<br>- Convention of Materiality<br>- Convention of Full Disclosure |

### Q2. [5 Marks] Explain the Convention of Conservatism (Prudence) and its Practical Accounting Applications.
**Model Answer:**
### Convention of Conservatism (Prudence)
**Core Maxim:** *'Anticipate no profit, but provide for all possible foreseeable losses.'*

### Practical Applications in Final Accounts:
1. **Valuation of Closing Stock**: Valued at **Cost or Net Realizable Value (Market Value), whichever is lower**.
2. **Provision for Doubtful Debts**: Created against debtors to anticipate future bad debts before they actually default.
3. **Provision for Discount on Debtors**: Estimated and debited to P&L.
4. **Non-recognition of Unrealized Capital Gains**: Assets are never appreciated upward on balance sheet based on market rumors.

### Q3. [5 Marks] Explain the Three-Column Cash Book Structure and Contra Entries.
**Model Answer:**
A Three-Column Cash Book serves as both journal and ledger for cash transactions, containing columns for **Discount**, **Cash**, and **Bank** on both Debit (Receipts) and Credit (Payments) sides.

### Handling Contra Entries (`C`):
1. **Cash Deposited into Bank**: Debit Bank Column (receipt in bank) and Credit Cash Column (cash going out).
2. **Cash Withdrawn from Bank for Office Use**: Debit Cash Column (cash coming in) and Credit Bank Column (payment from bank).

*Note:* Discount columns are never balanced against each other; their totals are posted directly to Discount Allowed (debit) and Discount Received (credit) ledger accounts.

### Q4. [5 Marks] Differentiate between Trade Discount and Cash Discount.
**Model Answer:**
| Feature | Trade Discount | Cash Discount |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Allowed to encourage bulk purchasing | Allowed to encourage prompt/early payment |
| **Timing** | Deducted at the time of purchase/invoice | Allowed at the time of settling cash payment |
| **Accounting Entry** | Deducted from invoice price; **NO entry** in ledger | Recorded explicitly in **Discount Account** |
| **Condition** | Linked to sales volume | Linked to payment within credit period |

### Q3. [10/15 Marks] Explain the Causes of Disagreement between Cash Book and Pass Book and the Method of Preparing a Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS).
**Comprehensive University Solution:**
### Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)
BRS reconciles the bank balance shown in the company's Cash Book with the balance shown in the Bank Pass Book / Bank Statement on a specific date.

### Major Causes of Disagreement:
1. **Timing Differences**:
   - *Cheques issued by firm but not yet presented for payment*: Bank balance higher than cash book.
   - *Cheques paid into bank but not yet credited/collected*: Cash book higher than bank.
2. **Transactions Initiated by Bank**:
   - *Direct collections by bank (interest, dividends)*: Bank higher.
   - *Direct debits by bank (bank charges, standing orders, loan EMI)*: Bank lower.
3. **Errors**: Wrong debit/credit posted by firm's cashier or bank clearing house.

### Model Reconciliation Format (Starting with Cash Book Debit Balance):

| Particulars | Amount (Add) | Amount (Less) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
