## 📌 Section A: High-Yield Very Short Answer Questions (2 Marks Each)
*Target: Write precise, definition-focused answers in 2–3 sentences or structured tables to secure maximum marks.*

### Q1. [2 Marks] What is the primary objective of preparing a Trial Balance?
**Model Answer:**
To test and verify the arithmetical accuracy of ledger postings. If the sum of debit balances equals the sum of credit balances, it provides preliminary proof that double entry was maintained.

### Q2. [2 Marks] Name four types of errors that are NOT disclosed by a Trial Balance.
**Model Answer:**
1. **Errors of Omission** (transaction completely omitted from books).
2. **Errors of Commission** (correct amount posted to wrong account on correct side).
3. **Errors of Principle** (violating capital vs revenue expenditure principles).
4. **Compensating Errors** (two errors canceling out each other).

### Q3. [2 Marks] What is a Suspense Account?
**Model Answer:**
A temporary clearing account opened to absorb the difference when a Trial Balance fails to balance, allowing final accounts to be drafted while errors are investigated and rectified.

### Q4. [2 Marks] What does the Trading Account primarily ascertain?
**Model Answer:**
The Trading Account matches Net Sales with the Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) to determine **Gross Profit** (if Sales > COGS) or **Gross Loss** (if COGS > Sales).

### Q5. [2 Marks] State the formula for Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).
**Model Answer:**
$$\text{COGS} = \text{Opening Stock} + \text{Net Purchases} + \text{Direct Factory Expenses} - \text{Closing Stock}$$

### Q6. [2 Marks] How is an Outstanding Expense treated in final accounts when given in adjustments?
**Model Answer:**
1. Added to the respective expense on the debit side of Trading/P&L Account.
2. Shown on the **Liabilities side** of the Balance Sheet under Current Liabilities.

### Q7. [2 Marks] How is Closing Stock treated when it appears INSIDE the Trial Balance?
**Model Answer:**
When Closing Stock is inside the Trial Balance, it has already been adjusted into Purchases; hence it appears **ONLY on the Asset side** of the Balance Sheet.

### Q8. [2 Marks] Differentiate between Straight Line Method (SLM) and Written Down Value (WDV) of depreciation.
**Model Answer:**
- **SLM**: Fixed annual depreciation computed on original cost; asset book value can reduce to zero.
- **WDV**: Decreasing annual depreciation computed on written-down book value; asset book value never technically reaches zero.

## 📌 Section B: Conceptual Short Answer Questions (5 Marks Each)
*Target: Provide structured answers in 150–200 words with headings, block diagrams, and comparison matrices.*

### Q1. [5 Marks] Explain Errors Disclosed vs Errors Not Disclosed by a Trial Balance.
**Model Answer:**
### 1. Errors Disclosed (Trial Balance Disagrees)
- Posting an entry to only one side of an account.
- Posting different amounts on debit and credit sides.
- Wrong totaling of subsidiary books or ledger accounts.
- Omitting an account balance from the trial balance.
- Entering a debit balance in the credit column of trial balance.

### 2. Errors Not Disclosed (Trial Balance Agrees Perfectly)
- **Complete Omission**: Invoice lost; neither debit nor credit entered.
- **Error of Principle**: Purchased machinery debited to Repairs Account.
- **Compensating Error**: Over-debiting Cash by Rs. 500 and under-crediting Sales by Rs. 500.
- **Error of Original Entry**: Transaction entered as Rs. 4,500 instead of Rs. 5,400 in journal.

### Q2. [5 Marks] Explain Marshalling of Assets and Liabilities in the Balance Sheet.
**Model Answer:**
Marshalling refers to the systematic arrangement of assets and liabilities in the Balance Sheet in a logical order:

### 1. Order of Liquidity (Common for Sole Traders & Partnerships)
- **Assets**: Most liquid first $\implies$ Cash in Hand $\to$ Cash at Bank $\to$ Marketable Securities $\to$ Debtors $\to$ Stock $\to$ Furniture $\to$ Plant $\to$ Goodwill.
- **Liabilities**: Most urgent first $\implies$ Bank Overdraft $\to$ Creditors $\to$ Bills Payable $\to$ Bank Loans $\to$ Capital.

### 2. Order of Permanence (Standard for Joint Stock Companies)
- Permanent, fixed assets appear first, ending with cash.
- Long-term capital and debentures appear first, ending with current liabilities.

### Q3. [5 Marks] Explain the Concept and Treatment of Bad Debts and Provision for Doubtful Debts.
**Model Answer:**
### Comprehensive Bad Debts Accounting
1. **Further Bad Debts in Adjustments**:
   - Debit Profit & Loss Account.
   - Deduct from Sundry Debtors in Balance Sheet.
2. **Provision for Doubtful Debts (e.g., 5%)**:
   - Formula: $\text{Provision} = (\text{Debtors} - \text{Further Bad Debts}) \times \text{Rate}\%$.
   - Debited to P&L Account.
   - Deducted from Debtors on the Asset side of Balance Sheet.
3. **Provision for Discount on Debtors (e.g., 2%)**:
   - Computed on remaining *good* debtors: $(\text{Debtors} - \text{Bad Debts} - \text{Doubtful Debt Provision}) \times \text{Rate}\%$.
   - Debited to P&L and deducted in Balance Sheet.

### Q4. [5 Marks] Differentiate between Capital Expenditure, Revenue Expenditure, and Deferred Revenue Expenditure.
**Model Answer:**
| Dimension | Capital Expenditure | Revenue Expenditure | Deferred Revenue Expenditure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Benefit Period** | Multi-year long-term benefit | Consumed within current accounting year | Extends over 3–5 years but creates no physical asset |
| **Earning Capacity** | Increases earning capacity of business | Maintains existing operating capacity | Promotes brand awareness over multiple years |
| **Financial Statement** | Capitalized as Asset on Balance Sheet | Debited to Trading or P&L Account | Expensed gradually; unamortized part shown on B/S |
| **Example** | Purchase of machinery, factory building | Factory wages, office rent, repairs | Heavy advertising for new product launch |

### Q2. [10/15 Marks] Explain the Complete Procedure for Rectification of Errors (Before and After Preparation of Trial Balance) with Journal Entries.
**Comprehensive University Solution:**
### Rectification of Accounting Errors

### Case 1: Rectification Before Trial Balance Preparation
Single-sided errors are rectified directly by entering explanatory notes in the affected ledger account:
- E.g., Sales Book undercast by Rs. 1,000 $\implies$ Credit Sales Account with: *'By undercasting of Sales Book: Rs. 1,000'*.

### Case 2: Rectification After Trial Balance (Using Suspense Account)
1. **Purchase of furniture for Rs. 5,000 was debited to Purchases Account**:
   - *Rectification Entry:* Debit Furniture A/c Rs. 5,000, Credit Purchases A/c Rs. 5,000 (Error of Principle).
2. **Sales Book was undercast by Rs. 2,000**:
   - *Rectification Entry:* Debit Suspense A/c Rs. 2,000, Credit Sales A/c Rs. 2,000.
3. **Payment of Rs. 3,500 to Mohan was posted to his account as Rs. 5,300**:
   - Excess debit of Rs. 1,800.
   - *Rectification Entry:* Debit Suspense A/c Rs. 1,800, Credit Mohan A/c Rs. 1,800.
4. **Credit sale of Rs. 8,000 to Rohit was completely omitted from books**:
   - *Rectification Entry:* Debit Rohit A/c Rs. 8,000, Credit Sales A/c Rs. 8,000 (Error of Omission).

### Q3. [10/15 Marks] Explain the Two Primary Methods of Calculating Depreciation (SLM vs WDV) with Mathematical Comparisons.
**Comprehensive University Solution:**
### SLM vs WDV Mathematical Analysis

**Example Scenario:** Asset Cost = Rs. 1,00,000, Scrap Value = Rs. 10,000, Useful Life = 5 years. Depreciation rate under WDV = 20%.

### 1. Straight Line Method (SLM)
$$\text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Scrap Value}}{\text{Useful Life}} = \frac{1,00,000 - 10,000}{5} = \text{Rs. } 18,000 \text{ per year}$$
- Depreciation is uniform every year.

### 2. Written Down Value Method (WDV)
- Year 1: $1,00,000 \times 20\% = \text{Rs. } 20,000$. Ending Book Value = Rs. 80,000.
- Year 2: $80,000 \times 20\% = \text{Rs. } 16,000$. Ending Book Value = Rs. 64,000.
- Year 3: $64,000 \times 20\% = \text{Rs. } 12,800$. Ending Book Value = Rs. 51,200.
- Year 4: $51,200 \times 20\% = \text{Rs. } 10,240$. Ending Book Value = Rs. 40,960.
- Year 5: $40,960 \times 20\% = \text{Rs. } 8,192$. Ending Book Value = Rs. 32,768.

### Key Advantage of WDV:
As the machine ages, **Repairs & Maintenance expenses increase**. Under WDV, depreciation charges decrease. The combined burden on P&L (Depreciation + Repairs) remains roughly constant over time.
