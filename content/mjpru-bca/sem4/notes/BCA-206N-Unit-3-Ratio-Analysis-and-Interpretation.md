### Q1. [2 Marks] What is Ratio Analysis?
**Model Answer:**
Ratio Analysis is the mathematical technique of analyzing financial statements by computing quantitative ratios between figures to evaluate liquidity, solvency, efficiency, and profitability.

### Q2. [2 Marks] What is the standard ideal norm for the Current Ratio?
**Model Answer:**
The universally accepted benchmark standard for Current Ratio is **2:1** (Rs. 2 of Current Assets for every Rs. 1 of Current Liabilities).

### Q3. [2 Marks] How is Quick Ratio (Acid-Test Ratio) calculated?
**Model Answer:**
$$\text{Quick Ratio} = \frac{\text{Quick Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventory (Stock)} - \text{Prepaid Expenses}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$
Ideal benchmark is **1:1**.

### Q4. [2 Marks] Define Debt-to-Equity Ratio and state its ideal standard.
**Model Answer:**
$$\text{Debt-Equity Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Long-Term Debt}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity (Net Worth)}}$$
Ideal benchmark standard is **2:1**.

### Q5. [2 Marks] State the formula for Gross Profit Ratio and Net Profit Ratio.
**Model Answer:**
- $\text{Gross Profit Ratio} = (\text{Gross Profit} / \text{Net Revenue from Operations}) \times 100$
- $\text{Net Profit Ratio} = (\text{Net Profit after Tax} / \text{Net Revenue from Operations}) \times 100$

### Q6. [2 Marks] What does the Stock Turnover Ratio measure?
**Model Answer:**
It measures how many times average inventory is sold and replaced during an accounting period:
$$\text{Stock Turnover Ratio} = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold}}{\text{Average Inventory}}$$

### Q7. [2 Marks] How is the Average Collection Period (in days) calculated?
**Model Answer:**
$$\text{Average Collection Period} = \frac{365}{\text{Debtors Turnover Ratio}} = \frac{\text{Average Trade Debtors} \times 365}{\text{Net Credit Sales}}$$

### Q8. [2 Marks] Explain Interest Coverage Ratio.
**Model Answer:**
$$\text{Interest Coverage Ratio} = \frac{\text{EBIT (Earnings Before Interest & Tax)}}{\text{Fixed Interest Charges}}$$
Higher ratio indicates greater margin of safety for lenders.

### Q1. [5 Marks] Classify Accounting Ratios into Four Functional Categories with Formulas.
**Model Answer:**
### Classification of Accounting Ratios

| Category | Objective | Core Key Ratios |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Liquidity Ratios** | Test ability to meet short-term maturing obligations | - Current Ratio: $CA / CL$ (Norm 2:1)<br>- Quick Ratio: $(CA - \text{Stock} - \text{Prepaid}) / CL$ (Norm 1:1)<br>- Absolute Cash Ratio: $(\text{Cash} + \text{Bank} + \text{Securities}) / CL$ |
| **2. Solvency / Leverage** | Test long-term financial stability and capital structure | - Debt-Equity Ratio: $\text{Long Debt} / \text{Equity}$ (Norm 2:1)<br>- Proprietary Ratio: $\text{Shareholders' Funds} / \text{Total Assets}$<br>- Interest Coverage Ratio: $EBIT / \text{Interest}$ |
| **3. Activity / Efficiency** | Measure operational velocity of asset utilization | - Stock Turnover: $COGS / \text{Avg Stock}$<br>- Debtors Turnover: $\text{Credit Sales} / \text{Avg Debtors}$<br>- Creditors Turnover: $\text{Credit Purchases} / \text{Avg Creditors}$<br>- Working Capital Turnover: $\text{Sales} / \text{Working Capital}$ |
| **4. Profitability Ratios** | Evaluate operating efficiency and financial returns | - Gross Profit Ratio: $(GP / \text{Sales}) \times 100$<br>- Net Profit Ratio: $(NP / \text{Sales}) \times 100$<br>- ROCE (ROI): $(EBIT / \text{Capital Employed}) \times 100$<br>- ROE: $(\text{Net Profit after Tax} / \text{Equity}) \times 100$<br>- EPS: $\text{Earnings for Equity} / \text{Number of Shares}$ |

### Q2. [5 Marks] Explain the DuPont Analysis Framework for Return on Equity (ROE).
**Model Answer:**
### DuPont 3-Step Decomposition of ROE
DuPont analysis decomposes Return on Equity into three operational drivers:

$$\text{ROE} = \text{Net Profit Margin} \times \text{Asset Turnover} \times \text{Financial Leverage}$$

$$\text{ROE} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Sales}} \right) \times \left( \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Total Assets}} \right) \times \left( \frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity}} \right)$$

1. **Profit Margin (Operating Efficiency)**: How much profit is generated per rupee of sales.
2. **Asset Turnover (Asset Use Efficiency)**: How productively assets generate revenues.
3. **Financial Leverage (Equity Multiplier)**: The proportion of debt used to fund assets.

### Q3. [5 Marks] Explain Limitations of Ratio Analysis in Financial Decision Making.
**Model Answer:**
1. **Historical Cost Distortion**: Ratios ignore price-level changes and inflation.
2. **Qualitative Factors Ignored**: Ratios omit employee morale, customer loyalty, management integrity.
3. **Differences in Accounting Policies**: One firm using SLM and another WDV makes ratios non-comparable.
4. **Window Dressing**: Management can manipulate end-of-year balances to artificially flatter ratios.
5. **Lack of Universal Benchmarks**: A 2:1 current ratio may be ideal for retail but excessive for cash-rich utilities.

### Q4. [5 Marks] Explain Operating Ratio vs Operating Profit Ratio.
**Model Answer:**
- **Operating Ratio**: Percentage of sales absorbed by operating costs:
  $$\text{Operating Ratio} = \frac{\text{COGS} + \text{Operating Expenses}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100$$
- **Operating Profit Ratio**: Percentage of sales remaining as operational profit:
  $$\text{Operating Profit Ratio} = \frac{\text{Operating Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100$$
- **Key Identity**: $\mathbf{\text{Operating Ratio} + \text{Operating Profit Ratio} = 100\%}$.

### Q1. [10/15 Marks] Solve a Comprehensive Numerical Problem Calculating Liquidity, Turnover, Solvency, and Profitability Ratios.
**Comprehensive University Solution:**
### University Examination Model Numerical Solution

**Financial Data of Apex Enterprises for Year 2023–24:**
- Net Sales (80% on credit): Rs. 20,00,000
- Gross Profit: Rs. 5,00,000
- Net Profit after Tax: Rs. 2,00,000
- Operating Expenses: Rs. 1,00,000
- Opening Stock: Rs. 1,30,000 | Closing Stock: Rs. 1,70,000
- Sundry Debtors: Rs. 2,00,000 | Bills Receivable: Rs. 50,000
- Current Assets (Total): Rs. 6,00,000
- Current Liabilities: Rs. 3,00,000
- 12% Long-Term Debt: Rs. 4,00,000
- Shareholders' Funds (Equity): Rs. 6,00,000

---

### 1. Liquidity Ratios
- **Current Ratio**:
  $$CR = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}} = \frac{6,00,000}{3,00,000} = \mathbf{2.0 : 1} \quad (\text{Ideal})$$
- **Quick Ratio**:
  $$\text{Quick Assets} = 6,00,000 - 1,70,000 \text{ (Stock)} = 4,30,000$$
  $$QR = \frac{4,30,000}{3,00,000} = \mathbf{1.43 : 1}$$

### 2. Profitability Ratios
- **Gross Profit Ratio**:
  $$GP \% = \left( \frac{5,00,000}{20,00,000} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{25\%}$$
- **Net Profit Ratio**:
  $$NP \% = \left( \frac{2,00,000}{20,00,000} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{10\%}$$
- **Operating Ratio**:
  $$COGS = \text{Sales} - GP = 20,00,000 - 5,00,000 = Rs. 15,00,000$$
  $$\text{Operating Ratio} = \frac{15,00,000 + 1,00,000}{20,00,000} \times 100 = \frac{16,00,000}{20,00,000} \times 100 = \mathbf{80\%}$$
- **Operating Profit Ratio**: $100\% - 80\% = \mathbf{20\%}$.

### 3. Activity / Turnover Ratios
- **Stock Turnover Ratio**:
  $$\text{Average Stock} = \frac{1,30,000 + 1,70,000}{2} = Rs. 1,50,000$$
  $$STR = \frac{15,00,000}{1,50,000} = \mathbf{10 \text{ times}}$$
- **Debtors Turnover Ratio**:
  $$\text{Net Credit Sales} = 80\% \text{ of } 20,00,000 = Rs. 16,00,000$$
  $$\text{Trade Receivables} = 2,00,000 + 50,000 = Rs. 2,50,000$$
  $$DTR = \frac{16,00,000}{2,50,000} = \mathbf{6.4 \text{ times}}$$
- **Average Collection Period**:
  $$\text{Collection Days} = \frac{365}{6.4} = \mathbf{57 \text{ days}}$$

### 4. Solvency Ratio
- **Debt-Equity Ratio**:
  $$DER = \frac{\text{Long-Term Debt}}{\text{Shareholders' Funds}} = \frac{4,00,000}{6,00,000} = \mathbf{0.67 : 1} \quad (\text{Very Safe, Low Gearing})$$

### Q2. [10/15 Marks] Reconstruct the Balance Sheet from Given Financial Ratios (Reverse Engineering Problem).
**Comprehensive University Solution:**
### Reconstructing Financial Statements from Ratios
**Given Information:**
- Working Capital: Rs. 1,20,000
- Current Ratio: 2.5 : 1
- Liquid Ratio: 1.5 : 1
- Proprietary Ratio (Fixed Assets to Capital): 0.75
- Bank Overdraft: Nil

### Step-by-Step Mathematical Solution:
1. **Calculate Current Liabilities and Current Assets**:
   $$Working Capital = CA - CL$$
   $$2.5(CL) - CL = 1,20,000 \implies 1.5(CL) = 1,20,000$$
   $$\mathbf{CL = Rs. 80,000}$$
   $$\mathbf{CA = 2.5 \times 80,000 = Rs. 2,00,000}$$

2. **Calculate Liquid Assets and Closing Stock**:
   $$\text{Liquid Assets} = 1.5 \times CL = 1.5 \times 80,000 = Rs. 1,20,000$$
   $$\mathbf{Stock} = CA - \text{Liquid Assets} = 2,00,000 - 1,20,000 = \mathbf{Rs. 80,000}$$

3. **Calculate Capital and Fixed Assets**:
   Assume Capital Employed equals Proprietary Funds:
   If $Fixed Assets / Capital = 0.75$, then Working Capital constitutes remaining $0.25$ of Capital.
   $$\text{Working Capital} = 0.25 \times Capital = 1,20,000$$
   $$\mathbf{Capital = 1,20,000 / 0.25 = Rs. 4,80,000}$$
   $$\mathbf{Fixed Assets = 0.75 \times 4,80,000 = Rs. 3,60,000}$$

4. **Reconstructed Balance Sheet**:
   - **Assets**: Fixed Assets (Rs. 3,60,000) + Current Assets (Rs. 2,00,000) = **Rs. 5,60,000**.
   - **Liabilities**: Capital (Rs. 4,80,000) + Current Liabilities (Rs. 80,000) = **Rs. 5,60,000**.

### Q3. [10/15 Marks] Explain How Ratio Analysis is Utilized by Different Stakeholders (Management, Investors, Bankers, Creditors).
**Comprehensive University Solution:**
### Stakeholder Perspectives on Financial Ratios
1. **Short-Term Creditors & Suppliers**:
   - Primary concern: **Immediate Liquidity**.
   - Key ratios: Current Ratio (2:1), Quick Ratio (1:1), Cash Ratio, Creditors Turnover.

   - Primary concern: **Operational Efficiency & Cost Control**.
